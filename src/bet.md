## The bet

<!-- One paragraph. What I believe will be true in ten years, stated so that it could turn out false. -->
I believe that LLMs will continue to improve, yet in a highly jagged way, that is highly dependant on the availability of RLVR settings. As a result, LLMs will *not* become automatic researchers making discoveries in the *empirical sciences*, where massive counterfactual datasets do not exist. 

However, LLMs will massively lower the barrier to entry for *any* scientific domain, meaning anyone with a domain-agnostic vision can enter and disrupt any domain. This is because (1) an expert can navigate a different domain by staying at a high level while steering the LLM to fill in the details; (2) an expert can use the LLMs as a  tool to massively accelerate the learning process.

I will pioneer a new field, **AI for Science** (not *AI for Biology*, not *AI for Physics*), that asks fundamental questions that cut across domains (akin to Philosophy of Science), and uses computational tools to solve them.

Due to this, the impact that an *insightful generalist* can have will continue to grow exponentially, and the most high-impact investment I can make as an AI for Science researcher in the early days of the field blossoming is two-fold:

1. **Mastery of the learning process**: I need to continue thinking hard, refining, and practicing the skill of entering a field I've never studied with a high-level vision in mind, absorb the relevant knowledge and skills in the most efficient way possible, and continue learning while working on the specific research project.
2. **Defining what the field AI for Science is for**: Currently, there are many specific fields, such as AI for Biology, AI for Physics, AI for Chemistry, but not yet a field that is more generically, "AI for Science". I need to present a compelling vision for what that is. Note that this requires an appreciation for the conversations that have been happening in the field of Philosophy of Science.
