## Questions I'm holding

Written before reading each chapter, and left as written.

**1. The Reach of Explanations.**

1. Deutsch will argue that knowledge is not derived from experience. Where, precisely, does observation enter his account, and what is it *for* if not a source? Watch what work the word "conjecture" does.
2. Before reading: write your own one-sentence criterion for what makes an explanation good. After: compare it with his. Could his criterion be computed for a model? Is a trained network a good explanation of its training data by his standard, or the opposite?
3. He will claim good explanations have *reach*: they explain more than they were built to explain. Is reach the same thing as out-of-distribution generalization, or does the resemblance break somewhere? Find where.
4. What would count as evidence against "induction is a myth"? Does a model that generalizes from data count, or would he say that isn't what he means? Predict his move before you read it.
